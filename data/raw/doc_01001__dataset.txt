A blockchain is a distributed digital ledger that records transactions across many computers so that no single record can be altered without changing all subsequent blocks and gaining agreement from the network. Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data, forming a tamper-resistant chain. Blockchains underpin cryptocurrencies but are also explored for supply chain tracking, digital identity, and smart contracts. Public blockchains are open to anyone, while private or permissioned blockchains restrict participation to approved members. Scalability and energy consumption remain active areas of research and debate.
